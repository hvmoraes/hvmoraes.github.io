About

Engineer first.
Now on the other side of the table.

I joined Nokia writing C++ for 5G cloud baseband software. Then I ran the sprints. Now I help shape the product. Three roles in under two years, all on the same product area, which means I know exactly what I'm asking my teams to build.

Day to day I own feature selection and backlog prioritization across two R&D engineering teams. I run effort estimation and risk assessment, translate customer and system requirements into work engineers can actually start, and keep Scrum Masters, architects and system engineers pointed in the same direction.

The part I did not expect to love is the human one. Building software taught me how to solve technical problems. Building products is teaching me which problems are worth solving, and that the plan is the easy part. The real work is the relationships. Staying close to people, knowing where they are stuck and what they need, is what keeps a plan alive.

Having written this kind of code myself helps. When an engineer tells me something is harder than it looks, I usually know why. When an estimate looks too optimistic, I can usually tell before the sprint does.

Where I'm heading: customer facing technical work. Technical account management, customer engagement, solutions roles. Places where the depth is the credibility and the relationship is the job.
